Since roughly late August 2026, Caesey has been exploring an early-stage company: take commercially usable
open-weight models (Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM/Z.ai, Kimi/Moonshot, Nemotron, gpt-oss, Mistral, Llama), operate them
himself on Australian GPU infrastructure, and sell inference access to Australian customers. Shorthand:
"Global open weights. Australian compute. Australian company. Australian customers." Working directory /
possible name: OPEN INFERENCE.

Key positions held as of 2026-08-29:
- Wants to be a model **operator**, not a reseller of someone else's inference API.
- Does **not** intend to own GPUs initially — rent from Australian neoclouds (Firmus, Amaze, Navigate Network,
  Sharon AI, Micron21/mCloud; AWS Sydney as the hyperscaler comparison). Micron21 published ~A$4k/month H200 as a
  reference price point. No quotes obtained yet.
- Sees models as "inventory"; interested in day-zero Australian availability of major open-weight releases, and
  in a future market for hosting *derivative* checkpoints (fine-tunes, post-trains, customer-owned models).
- Treats per-model licence review as an operations function; wants a model registry (version, hash, licence,
  commercial rights, hardware reqs, deploy config).
- Wants data-residency claims to be literally true (prompts, logs, backups, weights, support access, telemetry,
  DR, subprocessors), not marketing.
- Competitor/adjacent read: Southern Cross AI (SCX) broader than this idea; Amaze is both a potential competitor
  and a potential supplier; Fireworks×Firmus proves an inference operator can sit above an AU compute supplier.

**Nothing is decided**: no company, no infra, no customers, no capacity, no first model, no pricing, no vertical,
no name. He explicitly wants assumptions challenged and evidence to change the shape.
